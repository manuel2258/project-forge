The progress bar is segmented by item, not by decision. Six green ticks under one wide segment is the interruption you just cleared; the single segments after it are the stuck ticket and this approval. You can see both how far through you are and how lumpy the rest is.
Every kind renders in the sequence, not just questions — an approval, a stuck ticket and a review-ready ticket are each one screen. That is what makes it a pass over the queue rather than a question wizard bolted onto one card.
A focused screen can afford context the card cannot. The approval screen states what the work cost to reach this point, which is the fact most likely to change your answer, and links to the document. On a three-line card there is no room for it; here there is nothing else competing.
The fast path stays on the list. Accepting six recommendations, approving a version or taking the obvious transition is one tap from the skim without entering the sequence at all — so the sequence is for the decisions that deserve attention, not a toll on the ones that do not.

Implementation plan

The engine admits a ticket, walks the workflow from its initial state, and creates one job per job-state entry. Each job is given a pinned manifest and may not read anything outside it.

Retries are capped per transition. When the cap is exhausted and the ticket sticks, the engine stops producing jobs and waits for an operator decision.

Pending work is persisted outside the BEAM so a restart does not lose pending work, and resumed on boot from the same manifest.

Spend accumulates per job and sums per ticket. Reaching the ceiling sticks the ticket rather than cancelling it, so no work is lost.

The timeline replaces the flat stack. Each state is one step carrying its own jobs, outcomes, cost and artifacts, so the question “what happened, and what did it cost” is answered by reading downward instead of correlating four separate sections.
Unreached states are drawn, dimmed. The lifecycle is project-defined and the operator should be able to see where this ticket is going without opening the workflow file. It also makes a terminal state visibly terminal — the rail stops.
Stuck is drawn on the step, never as a step. The ochre dot marks the state the ticket is in; the flag sits beside the state name. There is no “stuck” row in the timeline because there is no such state.
The decision is hoisted above the timeline. If the page has an answerable action it appears once, at the top, in the same ochre as the queue mark. Everything below it is reference.
Declared outcome is set in the outcome colour and never abbreviated. did_not_converge and tests_failed are the most informative words on the page, so they get a column of their own in the jobs table rather than a parenthesis.
Approval state rides on the artifact chip, with the version in it. plan.md v2 ✓ approved is one object; when v3 is written the chip reads v3 ◇ unapproved on its own. The word “latest” appears nowhere in the design.
Absorbs §7 how. Job hierarchy: the jobs table inside a step indents children under parents — no new layout. Blobs and presentations: new chip types in the same row, with presentation chips drawn without any approval slot so they cannot read as approvable. Experimental results: a chip variant with a hollow stroke and the word experimental, never a ✓. Merge approval: the review_ready step's action opens the new dense view; the timeline is what that view summarises down its side.

The waiting figure is the second-largest thing on a card. It is the key the list is sorted by; setting it in mono at 17px, right-aligned, lets you read the age of the whole queue down one edge without reading a word.
Urgency is one ochre mark, not a palette. Filled ochre diamond = a job is held or the ticket is stuck. Hollow indigo square = a decision that spends money or advances state. Hollow green circle = routine. Eight kinds need only these three marks plus the label, so the list never becomes a colour quiz.
Free text is a peer of the options, not an escape hatch behind a link — it is a real field with its own Send, sitting at the foot of the same stack as the options.
The answered card stays in place and shows its send state inline with an Undo. It does not vanish on tap and it does not wait for the socket. This is the reliability story: the answer is durable on the device the moment you tap, the row tells you where it is, and it never silently disappears.
Non-blocking questions are a digest below the fold, under a heading that says they are not waiting on you. They are in the same ordered list — nothing is hidden — but they are prose, not cards, so they cannot compete with a held job.
The end of the queue is the success state. Reaching it is the point of the page, so it is marked in green and phrased as a completion, not an absence. The same block, alone, is the empty state.
Absorbs §7 how. Experiment requests and promotion decisions are the indigo mark with their own label and their own button row; analysis proposals are green. No new card shape is needed — only a label and a mark, which is the point of separating the three axes from the card kind.

07
Capacity, not cost
the cost model used on every surface in this document
One unit is one percent of a five-hour window, so a ticket's consumption is stated in windows — an absolute quantity that stays true when the work spans four of them. Usage accrues and never resets; capacity refills on a clock. They are drawn as two different objects because they answer two different questions: what did this cost and can anything run right now.
CAPACITY — LIVE, REFILLS
62% of this window resets 3h 41m
31% of the week resets Mon 00:00
Twenty ticks, one per five percent, so the window reads as a gauge rather than a progress bar — you are not making progress through it, you are consuming it. The week is thinner and plain: it is the ceiling behind the ceiling, and it only becomes the headline when it is the binding one.
